# Theory: what a coherence reward can and cannot identify

This note states the formal claims behind the coherence-gated reward, with
proofs, and records two findings from the synthetic-policy suite
(`ccrl train reward-suite`) that changed the reward relative to the original plan.
Notation follows `src/ccrl/reward/coherence.py`.

## 1. Setup

* Candidates `D`, `|D| = K`. Context `c` (age, sex, chief complaint).
  Evidence is a **set** `E ⊆ F` of atomic findings (a DDXPlus finding unit is
  a parent question with its descriptors; a MedCaseReasoning finding is one
  extracted sentence).
* A rendering `r(E, c; ω)` produces text; `ω` covers order (within
  exchangeable blocks), phrasing, verbatim re-statement of a finding, and
  insertion of certified-irrelevant sentences. A policy maps text to
  `q ∈ Δ^K`.
* Meaning-preserving group `𝒯`: transformations that leave `(E, c)` unchanged.
  Evidence-changing operations: `E ↦ E_r ⊂ E` (the default contrast is the
  triage set `E_r = {chief complaint}`), and swapping patients.

Assumptions (for Propositions 1 and 3 only):
**A1** findings are conditionally independent given the diagnosis;
**A2** prior `π(d|c)` and likelihoods `ℓ(f|d)` are fixed;
**A3** evidence is hard (observed with certainty).

## 2. Propositions

**Proposition 1 (order and duplication invariance; commutativity).** Under
A1–A3, for any `τ > 0` the (generalised) Bayes posterior

    q*_τ(d | E, c) ∝ π(d|c) · Π_{f ∈ E} ℓ(f|d)^τ

depends on `E` only as a set. Hence it is invariant to reordering, invariant to
duplication when the duplicate is a re-statement of the same set element, and
sequential updating gives the same result in any order.

*Proof.* The product over a finite set is commutative and associative, and a
set has no multiplicity, so `r(E;ω)` and `r(E;ω')` induce the same product.
Sequential updating with `E = E1 ∪ E2`, `E1 ∩ E2 = ∅`, multiplies the same
factors in a different order. ∎

*Caveats (stated in the paper).* If a duplicate were treated as a new,
conditionally independent observation, Bayes would double-count; the harness
therefore marks duplicates "As noted above". Order invariance fails for
Jeffrey conditionalisation (soft evidence), which is why invariance is only
imposed for hard, exchangeable findings (study rule 3.2). `τ = 1` is
ordinary naive Bayes; `τ < 1` is the tempered ("generalised" or "power")
posterior, which keeps every property above.

**Proposition 2 (non-identifiability of label-free coherence).** Let `σ` be any
permutation of `D` and let `R_coh` be any reward built only from
Jensen–Shannon divergences between output distributions and from their
Shannon entropies — in particular `I`, `S`, the sensitivity gate `G_S` and the
cross-patient information `M`. Then the relabelled policy `σ ∘ q*` receives
exactly the same `R_coh` as `q*`. More generally, for any likelihood family
`ℓ'` (not necessarily the true one), the policy `q'(·|E) ∝ π'(·)Π ℓ'(f|·)` is
exactly invariant under `𝒯`, so a reward built from invariance alone is
maximised by every such set-function posterior.

*Proof.* `JS(σp, σq) = JS(p, q)` and `H(σp) = H(p)`; the mean of relabelled
distributions is the relabelled mean, so `M` is unchanged. Every term is a
function of these quantities. The second statement follows from Proposition 1
applied to `ℓ'`. ∎

*Corollary.* Label-free coherence is a **necessary-condition regulariser**,
not an identification principle: it removes order-, phrasing- and
cue-dependence, but which posterior the policy settles on must come from
elsewhere (accuracy, fidelity, or the base model). This matches Andrews
(arXiv:2608.05015): coherence "does not restrict which objective rationalizes
behavior". Verified numerically: the label-permuted oracle obtains
`R_coh` identical to the oracle (difference < 1e-9).

**Proposition 3 (identification with update fidelity).** Under A1–A3, suppose
for every patient and every contrast `E_r ⊂ E` the policy satisfies

    q(·|E) ∝ q(·|E_r) · Π_{f ∈ E \ E_r} ℓ(f|·)^τ        (update fidelity)

and the empty-evidence output equals the prior, `q(·|∅, c) = π(·|c)`. Then
`q = q*_τ`. `R_fid = 1` exactly when the first condition holds on the sampled
contrasts. Combined with a bounded proper scoring rule on the label
(`R_acc`, Brier), the population maximiser of `α R_acc + γ R_fid` is `q*_τ`
when the data-generating process satisfies A1 with likelihood `ℓ^τ`.

*Proof sketch.* Chain the fidelity condition from `∅` to `E` through nested
contrasts; each step multiplies by the corresponding likelihood factor, and
the base case is the prior. Brier is strictly proper, so its expectation is
uniquely maximised by the true conditional distribution (Gneiting & Raftery
2007; the RLCR argument of Damani et al., arXiv:2507.16806). ∎

*Note.* DDXPlus was generated by a proprietary knowledge base and rule-based
system, so A1 holds only approximately; the corpus-likelihood posterior is
reported as a measured reference (`artifacts/frozen/ddxplus/reference.json`),
never assumed to be the generator.

**Proposition 4 (anti-collapse).**
(a) A constant policy has `M = 0`, hence `R_coh = 0`.
(b) A policy whose outputs take `k` distinct values has `M ≤ log k / log B`.
(c) A policy that ignores everything except the contrast set (e.g. answers
from the chief complaint alone) has zero floor-corrected movement between the
full rendering and the contrast, so `G_S = 0` (batch form) or `S_i = 0`
(per-rollout form), hence `R_coh = 0`.
(d) A purely stochastic policy has expected floor-corrected movement ≈ 0, so
`G_S ≈ 0`.

*Proof.* (a) `H(mean) − mean H = 0` when all rows are equal. (b) For a
uniform mixture over patients, `M·log B = I(patient; output) ≤ H(output) ≤ log k`.
(c) The full and contrast renderings give the same output, so the JS movement
equals the noise floor and the excess is 0. (d) The leave-one-out floor and
the movement have the same expectation under pure noise. ∎

## 3. Findings from the synthetic suite, and the two corrections

The suite (`python -m ccrl.reward.synthetic`, 1,024 DDXPlus training
patients, B = 16, G = 4) scores nine synthetic policies against the
corpus-reference oracle.

**Finding 1 — the reference posterior on DDXPlus is near-deterministic.**
With `τ` chosen by the proper scoring rule (minimum NLL of the true pathology
on training patients: `τ = 0.6`, NLL 0.026, top-1 0.98, entropy 0.026 nats),
removing ONE finding never moves the reference (median JS exactly 0), and
removing two thirds of the findings moves it in only ~10 % of patients. The
plan's sensitivity scale ("median Bayes ablation JS") is therefore 0, and a
single-finding sensitivity term rewards the ideal reasoner with nothing.
Only the triage contrast (chief complaint alone) moves the reference reliably
(median JS 0.56; > 0.05 for 67–85 % of patients).
**Change:** the default contrast `x⁻` is the triage rendering; the plan's
single-finding ablation remains (a) a panel evaluation transform and
(b) a reward option `contrast: single`.

**Correction 1 — noise-floor subtraction.** Raw distance from the contrast
rewards a stochastic policy for its own sampling noise. Every movement term
subtracts the rollout's leave-one-out retest floor. Check: the pure-noise
policy gets `R_coh = 0`.

**Finding 2 / Correction 2 — per-rollout sensitivity prefers over-reaction.**
Under redundant evidence a Bayesian reasoner correctly does not move for some
patients (for ~10 % of DDXPlus patients the chief complaint alone settles the
diagnosis). The plan's per-rollout form `R_coh = I·√S·gate` then penalises the
oracle on those patients, and the suite shows it **prefers a tempered,
over-reacting oracle (`q*^0.5`) to the Bayes oracle in total reward**
(2.29 vs 2.20). Making sensitivity a batch-level anti-collapse gate,
`G_S = √min(1, mean excess movement / δ_S)`, like `M`, removes the preference
while keeping every anti-collapse guarantee (Proposition 4). The per-rollout
form is kept as the ablation `abl_rollout_sensitivity`.

**Fidelity scale.** Distribution-space fidelity (JS between predicted and
actual) is too insensitive on near-one-hot posteriors (tempered oracle 0.999
vs oracle 0.9999). The implemented fidelity compares log-odds on the top-3
predicted diagnoses, with logits clipped to the 0.5–99.5 % output resolution,
and predicts only in the numerically stable direction
(reduced-evidence belief × likelihood of the added findings → full belief).

### Final suite result (batch gate; `δ_S = 0.234`, `m0 = 0.459`)

| policy | R_coh | R_fid | R_acc | R_total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| constant uniform | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.510 | 0.510 |
| constant prior | 0.000 | 0.003 | 0.508 | 0.509 |
| two-cluster (one bit) | 0.080 | 0.005 | 0.509 | 0.591 |
| chief complaint only | 0.000 | 0.334 | 0.707 | 0.873 |
| oracle, labels permuted | 1.000 | 0.023 | 0.084 | 1.095 |
| oracle tempered (q*^0.5) | 1.000 | 0.987 | 0.927 | 2.420 |
| **oracle** | **1.000** | **0.999** | **0.934** | **2.434** |
| oracle + order noise | 0.978 | 0.982 | 0.916 | 2.385 |
| pure noise | 0.000 | 0.009 | 0.479 | 0.484 |

All ten pass criteria hold (`R_coh(constant)=0`; cluster and one-finding below
the oracle; permuted = oracle exactly; noise < 10 % of the oracle; fidelity
of the oracle > 0.95 and above every other policy; oracle total above
permuted, tempered and order-noise). The margin over the tempered oracle is
small (≈0.6 %), which is the honest content of Proposition 2: everything that
separates Bayes from a coherent-but-miscalibrated reasoner comes from
fidelity and accuracy, not from coherence.

## 4. What this means for the claims

* "Label-free coherence training" is a regulariser that provably cannot
  collapse to constant, clustered, single-finding or noisy policies
  (Proposition 4), and provably cannot by itself pick the correct posterior
  (Proposition 2). Report it that way.
* The label-free arm (`ccr_only`, `labelfree_mcr`) is expected to improve
  invariance without improving — and must be tested for not harming —
  accuracy and direction agreement (non-inferiority, H5).
* Claims of moving *toward Bayes* belong to the arms with fidelity or
  accuracy (`ccr_acc_fid`, H6).
